There is no official Twitch sponsorship rate card. Twitch doesn't publish one, brands don't share theirs, and most "rates" articles either quote follower-based influencer math (the wrong metric for live) or give ranges so wide they're useless.

What *does* exist: published benchmark data from agencies and sponsorship marketplaces that price live streams the way buyers actually do — per concurrent-viewer-hour. This page collects those numbers with sources, so you can price from data instead of guessing. Every figure below links to where it comes from.

## The headline numbers

- **$0.60–$1.50 per concurrent-viewer-hour ([CPVH](/blog/what-is-cpvh))** is the published benchmark band for live-stream sponsorships; some agency sources cite rates up to **~$2.00** for agency-represented deals.[^1][^2]
- For mid-tier streamers — roughly **100–5,000 average concurrent viewers** — that math produces the deal sizes seen in the wild: **$500–$5,000 per sponsored stream**.[^1]
- Agencies handling this negotiation for creators typically charge **15–20% of deal value**.[^3]

So a streamer averaging 800 CCV doing a 3-hour sponsored stream is looking at a benchmark range of roughly **$1,440–$3,600** (800 × 3 × $0.60–$1.50). Follower count never enters the formula — live sponsorships are priced on who's actually in the room while the sponsorship runs.

## Rates by channel size

All rows below are the same published band applied at different audience sizes, assuming a 3-hour sponsored stream — shown so you can find your row, not because different bands apply at different sizes:

| Avg CCV | Viewer-hours (3h stream) | Benchmark range ($0.60–$1.50 CPVH) |
|---|---|---|
| 100 | 300 | $180 – $450 |
| 250 | 750 | $450 – $1,125 |
| 500 | 1,500 | $900 – $2,250 |
| 1,000 | 3,000 | $1,800 – $4,500 |
| 2,500 | 7,500 | $4,500 – $11,250 |
| 5,000 | 15,000 | $9,000 – $22,500 |

Two honest notes on reading this table. First, it's a **benchmark range, not a quote** — where a real deal lands depends on audience fit, deliverables, exclusivity, and the brand's budget. Second, the larger bands compound: at 2,500+ CCV, deals above the wild-observed $5,000 typically involve agency representation (where the ~$2.00 upper rates are cited) or multi-stream packages rather than a single stream at the top of the band.[^1]

Longer streams scale linearly: the same 500-CCV streamer doing a 4-hour sponsored stream is at 2,000 viewer-hours, so $1,200–$3,000.

## Why every "rates" article you've read disagrees

Search this keyword and you'll find figures from $50 to $50,000, which is why most streamers conclude rates are unknowable. The articles disagree because they're measuring different things:

- **Follower-based articles** import Instagram/TikTok influencer math ($X per 1,000 followers). Followers are the wrong denominator for live — a 500K-follower channel with 300 CCV and a 40K-follower channel with 800 CCV are, to a live sponsor, the second channel being worth more than twice the first.
- **Subscriber-revenue articles** are answering "how much do streamers earn on Twitch," which mixes subs, bits, and ads into figures that say nothing about what a *brand* pays for a *sponsorship*.
- **Anecdote articles** report single deals without audience sizes attached, which makes every number both true and useless.

The viewer-hour framing is the one buyers actually use, because it's the only one that measures what they're buying: attention-time. It's also directly comparable to the rest of a brand's media plan — 1,000 concurrent viewers for 3 hours is 3,000 hours of live attention with the product on screen, a unit a media buyer can put next to any other channel they're buying. That comparability is *why* agencies price this way, and why you should.

## Rates by deliverable

Most deals aren't a full dedicated stream. Published rate breakdowns price the common deliverables as fractions of your hourly rate:[^4]

| Deliverable | Typical pricing |
|---|---|
| Mid-stream ad read (30–90s) | The base unit for one-off integrations |
| Logo / overlay on screen | ~20–40% of hourly rate while displayed |
| Chat callouts + link drops | ~15–25% of hourly rate |
| Dedicated sponsored stream | Full CPVH math across all hours |
| VOD / video integration | Priced separately — persistent reach, usually with usage-rights terms |

Stack them. A "simple package" of overlay + two ad reads + pinned link is three billable line items, and a quote that itemizes them signals you've priced deals before.

## Rates by deal structure

Twitch sponsorships come in four shapes, and the shape changes what a "rate" even means:

- **Flat fee.** The benchmark case — CPVH math applies directly. Money moves brand → you, on terms you agreed before going live.
- **Affiliate / rev-share.** You're paid per conversion. Fair as a *bonus on top of* a flat fee; risky as the whole deal, because you carry all the performance risk and the brand carries none.
- **Bounty / CPA.** Paid per signup or install. Same caveat, sharper — and the community learned it the hard way. In widely-shared r/Twitch threads (298 and 795 upvotes), streamers documented bounty-style "sponsorships" through StreamElements where payment was revoked *after* campaigns completed — "Funny how they don't revoke it until it's over," as one affected streamer put it — with the top-voted comment concluding these "are not sponsorships, they are affiliate linking."[^5][^6] The episode ended with Razer's 2026 acquisition of StreamElements' assets, with part of the consideration reported as earmarked for unpaid creator payouts.[^5] If compensation depends entirely on tracked conversions, price it — and treat it — as an affiliate deal, not a sponsorship.
- **Hybrid.** Flat base + performance bonus. Increasingly common for mid-tier deals; benchmark the base with CPVH, then negotiate the bonus on top.

## What determines where you land in the band

Where a deal lands between $0.60 and $1.50 depends on things you can argue for:

1. **Audience fit** — an FPS-hardware brand in an FPS stream justifies the top of the band; category-endemic sponsors pay more because your room is full of their customers.
2. **Engagement** — chat velocity and returning-viewer share. Small, loyal audiences convert, and brands that have run live campaigns know it.
3. **Exclusivity and usage rights** — both are separate value, priced above the base rate, never conceded free.
4. **Multi-platform simulcast** — combined CCV across Twitch, YouTube Live, and Kick is your real audience number; count all of it.
5. **Proof of delivery** — streamers who send VOD timestamps and screenshots get renewed at better rates. Brands pay for certainty, and renewals are where rates climb.

## Do rates differ on YouTube Live, Kick, and TikTok Live?

The published band is drawn primarily from Twitch-centric agency data, but the unit it prices — a concurrent-viewer-hour of live attention — is platform-agnostic. Treat the band as your starting benchmark anywhere you stream, apply it to that platform's average CCV, and when you simulcast, quote on the combined number. Per-platform nuances (demographics, brand demand, ad-load norms) shift where in the band you argue for, not what the unit is.

## The uncomfortable part: getting the rate you agreed

Agreeing a rate is half the job. Across the creator economy, **48–87% of creators report being paid late** — 48% in Lumanu's survey of 500 influencers (with 38.5% of those waiting more than a month), and as high as 87% in payment-platform data cited by Campaign, which quotes a creator on Net-90 terms: "At what point does it just feel like unpaid work?"[^7][^8] Payment delays are creators' top complaint about brand deals.[^9]

Whatever rate you close: get deliverables, fee, and [payment terms](/blog/net-30-net-60-net-90) in writing before going live, invoice with a due date, and [follow up on a schedule when the date passes](/blog/sponsor-paying-late). A rate you can't collect isn't a rate — it's a receivable, and receivables need chasing.

## FAQ

*(FAQPage schema at publish)*

**How much do Twitch sponsorships pay?**
Published benchmarks price live sponsorships at $0.60–$1.50 per concurrent-viewer-hour. For mid-tier streamers (100–5,000 average CCV) that produces $500–$5,000 per sponsored stream, with agency-represented deals citing rates up to ~$2.00 per viewer-hour.

**How much can a small streamer with 100 viewers charge?**
At 100 average CCV, a 3-hour sponsored stream benchmarks at roughly $180–$450 (100 × 3 × $0.60–$1.50). Small-but-engaged audiences argue for the upper half of the band, especially with category-fit sponsors.

**Do sponsors pay by followers or viewers?**
Live sponsorships are priced on average concurrent viewers — the people in the room during the stream — not followers. Follower-based figures come from feed-content influencer math and misprice live channels in both directions.

**How much do Twitch streamers make from sponsorships per month?**
It depends on deal flow, not just rates: a 500-CCV streamer closing two $900–$2,250 sponsored streams a month grosses $1,800–$4,500 from sponsorships. The rate is the benchmark above; the volume is a pipeline question.

**Are StreamElements sponsorships worth it?**
Widely-shared community threads documented bounty-style StreamElements campaigns where payouts were revoked after completion, and the top-voted community verdict was that these function as affiliate linking rather than sponsorships. Read the linked threads and treat conversion-only deals accordingly: as affiliate offers, priced and agreed in writing as such.
