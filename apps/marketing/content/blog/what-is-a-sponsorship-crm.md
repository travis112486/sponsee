If you're closing more than one or two brand deals a year, at some point a deal stops living in your memory and starts living in a scattered pile of DMs, emails, and a spreadsheet you forgot to update. A sponsorship CRM is the tool built to stop that from happening. Here's what the term actually means, what the category looks like today, and whether you're far enough along to need one.

## Sponsorship CRM: a working definition

A **sponsorship CRM** (customer relationship management, borrowed from sales software) is a tool that tracks your brand-deal business end to end: which brands you're talking to and at what stage, what each deal is worth, what you agreed to deliver and by when, and whether you've actually been paid. The "customer" in a creator's CRM is the brand — the same relationship a sales rep tracks in Salesforce, just running the other direction.

It's a young category. The term itself is still being defined — which is part of why this page exists.

## What it actually does

For this comparison, we evaluate sponsorship CRMs across four jobs:

1. **Deal pipeline.** Every brand conversation in one place, at a visible stage (prospecting → negotiating → contracted → live → paid), instead of scattered across email threads and DMs you'd have to reconstruct from memory if a brand followed up in three weeks.
2. **Contract and deliverable tracking.** What you actually agreed to — the deliverable spec, usage rights, exclusivity window, payment terms — living somewhere other than a buried email (see the [contract checklist](/blog/sponsorship-contract-checklist) for the full list of what belongs here).
3. **Rate and pricing reference.** Some tool for [knowing whether a quote is fair](/blog/how-much-to-charge-sponsored-stream) before you accept it, instead of guessing or anchoring off whatever the brand offers first.
4. **Payment status and follow-up.** Knowing what's invoiced, what's due, what's overdue, and having a system — not a New Year's resolution — for following up before "slow" becomes "written off."

That's the bar we use to evaluate what's on the market below: a tool doing one or two of these might still be useful, but it's covering part of the job, not the whole thing.

## The squeeze creators are stuck in

Deal-ops tooling for brand sponsorships exists — just not built for the person actually doing the streaming. It comes from two directions, and mid-tier live streamers sit in the gap between them:

**From above:** brand-side platforms built for the companies writing the checks — Grin, Upfluence, CreatorIQ — run **$500–3,000+ per month** (Upfluence's floor alone is a reported $478/month), with annual lock-ins built around agency and enterprise procurement, not a solo creator's budget.[^29]

**From below:** talent agencies will run your deal-ops for you, but they take **15–20% of deal value** and generally want a roster of clients bigger than a single mid-tier streamer justifies.[^3]

Neither is built for someone running 100–5,000 average concurrent viewers (CCV) and closing sponsorships on their own — which is exactly the gap creator-side deal-ops tools have started filling.

## What's actually on the market today

A handful of creator-side tools have shown up in the last couple of years to close that gap, and they split into two groups. Most model a "deliverable" as something published once — a post, a video, a newsletter send — with no concept of a live segment. Two options aren't built that way, but come with their own tradeoffs: StreamElements has live-native bounty mechanics but carries documented trust problems, and talent agencies handle live sponsorships end to end but at a price most mid-tier streamers can't justify:

| Tool | Pricing | Deliverable model | Live mechanics (segments, overlays, CPVH) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Passionfroot | 5% commission on organic deals / 15% on network deals | Newsletter sends, videos, posts | None — no Twitch or Kick channel type[^124] |
| HeyKero | Free (beta) | Video production board, invoices | None — no rate-card intelligence[^125] |
| Creedn | $19/mo flat, 0% commission | YouTube/Instagram/TikTok posts | None[^126] |
| Nythor | $19–39/mo | Uploads, integrations, social posts | None[^127] |
| Beacons | $0–90/mo, 9% fee on the free tier | Media kit + invoicing, format-agnostic | None[^129] |
| StreamElements (Razer) | Free bounty marketplace | CPA/bounty campaigns | Live-native, but trust-damaged — the marketplace saw documented payment revocations before Razer's acquisition earmarked funds for unpaid creators[^77][^4] |
| Talent agencies | 15–20% of deal value | Full service | Yes — but priced out of the mid-tier[^3] |

None of that is a knock on what these tools are good at — Passionfroot and Beacons in particular have real traction with creators doing newsletter, video, and social sponsorships, and StreamElements' bounty mechanics and talent agencies' full-service model both work for the creators who can use them. But a live sponsorship is a different shape of deliverable than any of those: a 30–90-second mid-stream read, an overlay up for a segment, a chat callout, priced by concurrent viewers over time (cost per viewer-hour, or [CPVH](/blog/what-is-cpvh)) rather than a fixed impression count. The five creator-side tools in the table above don't model that at all. StreamElements and talent agencies come closer on the live-mechanics side, but neither pairs it with CPVH-style rate reference, contract tracking, and payment follow-up built for a solo mid-tier streamer's budget — which is the specific combination this category is missing.

## Who actually needs one

Not every creator with a sponsorship needs a dedicated tool for it. If you close one deal a year through a DM and a handshake, a CRM is overkill — a notes app is fine.

A sponsorship CRM starts earning its keep once you're a mid-tier live streamer with real sponsorship volume: roughly 100–5,000 CCV, several deals a quarter or more, and the unmanaged complexity that comes with it — payouts across multiple platforms, multiple 1099s at tax time, deliverables that need to happen on a live schedule, and rate-setting with no benchmark to check against. At that point, "I'll remember what we agreed to" stops being a plan.

## FAQ

*(FAQPage schema at publish)*

**What is a sponsorship CRM?**
A tool that tracks your brand-deal pipeline, contracts, deliverables, and payment status in one place — the same job a sales CRM does for a company's customer relationships, applied to a creator's brand relationships.

**Do I need a sponsorship CRM if I only do a few deals a year?**
Probably not yet. The category starts paying for itself once you're closing sponsorships regularly enough that tracking them in email and memory becomes the actual bottleneck — typically once you're a mid-tier streamer (100–5,000 CCV) doing multiple deals a quarter.

**What's the difference between a sponsorship CRM and a media-kit tool?**
A media kit is a pitch document — it helps a brand decide to work with you. A sponsorship CRM manages what happens after they say yes: the deal terms, the deliverable tracking, and getting paid.

**Are sponsorship CRMs free?**
Some are (HeyKero is free in beta; Beacons has a free tier with a 9% fee on it). Flat-fee paid tools generally run $19–39/month; commission-based tools take a cut of each deal instead of charging a subscription.

**Does any sponsorship CRM handle live-stream sponsorships specifically?**
Most creator-side tools on the market model deliverables as posts, videos, or newsletter sends, with no live-segment concept at all. StreamElements has live-native bounty mechanics, and talent agencies handle live deals end to end, but neither pairs that with CPVH-style rate reference and deal-pipeline tracking built for a solo mid-tier streamer.
