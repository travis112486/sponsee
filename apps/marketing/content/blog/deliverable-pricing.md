A brand doesn't usually ask for "a sponsored stream." They ask for an overlay *plus* two ad reads *plus* a pinned chat link *plus* maybe a VOD cutdown — and then offer one flat number for all of it. Is that a fair price for four things, or four favors priced as one?

Here's how to price each deliverable on its own, so you can look at a mixed offer and know whether it adds up.

## Start from your base rate

Every deliverable below prices as a fraction of your **hourly [CPVH](/blog/what-is-cpvh) rate** — average CCV × hours × the [published $0.60–$1.50-per-viewer-hour band](/blog/twitch-sponsorship-rates) (agency-repped deals cited up to ~$2.00).[^1][^2] If you haven't run that math yet, do it first; everything here is relative to that number, not a replacement for it.

## The deliverable table

Published rate breakdowns from agencies and sponsorship marketplaces put the common deliverables here, as fractions of your full hourly rate for the time they're active:[^3]

| Deliverable | Typical price | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Mid-stream ad read (30–90s) | The base unit | Most one-off deals are quoted off this |
| Logo / overlay on screen | ~20–40% of hourly rate | Priced for the hours it's actually displayed, not your whole stream length |
| Chat callouts / pinned link | ~15–25% of hourly rate | Cheap per-instance, but stacks fast if a brand wants several |
| Dedicated sponsored stream | Full CPVH math | The whole-stream case — see the [rates pillar](/blog/how-much-to-charge-sponsored-stream) |
| VOD / video integration | Priced separately | Persistent reach after you go offline — see below |

## Why overlays and callouts price as fractions, not flat fees

An overlay running for your whole 4-hour stream isn't the same product as a 60-second ad read — it's continuous, low-attention-cost exposure rather than one deliberate moment. Pricing it as a percentage of your hourly rate (rather than a flat "$50 for a logo") means the price scales with your actual audience size and stream length, the same way the full-stream rate does. A 150-CCV streamer's overlay and a 3,000-CCV streamer's overlay are not the same deliverable, even if both brands ask for "just a logo."

## VOD and usage rights are a different question

VOD integrations and clip usage aren't priced off the CPVH-per-hour model at all, because they're not time-bound — a brand wanting to rerun your clip in their own paid ads is licensing content, not sponsoring a stream. Two things to separate before you quote:

- **VOD reach on your own channel** (the sponsorship stays visible in your VOD after the stream ends) — price this as an add-on to the stream rate, since it extends the exposure window.
- **Usage rights** (the brand wants to reuse your clip/likeness in *their* marketing, outside your channel) — this is a licensing fee, negotiated separately, and scales with where they plan to run it (their socials vs. paid ad spend vs. broadcast).

Conflating the two is how streamers give away licensing for free inside a "sponsored stream" quote. If a brand's brief mentions "using the clip after," ask what for, and price it as its own line.

## Quoting a mixed offer

Once you know each deliverable's price, a mixed ask is just addition. If a brand wants an overlay for your 3-hour stream *plus* two ad reads *plus* a pinned chat link, price each line against your hourly rate and sum them — don't let "it's basically one ask" talk you into a single discounted number for four things.

**Write the quote itemized**, even if you're willing to bundle-discount at the end:

> "Overlay (3hr): $X · Ad read ×2: $Y · Chat callout: $Z · Total: $X+Y+Z, or $[bundled] if booked together."

Itemized quotes get negotiated line by line, which favors you — the brand can cut a line item they don't need instead of anchoring the whole deal down. A single bundled "$300 for everything" number gets accepted or rejected as one unit, and it tends to get accepted precisely because nobody can see what any piece is actually worth.

## FAQ

*(FAQPage schema at publish)*

**How much should I charge for a logo overlay on stream?**
Roughly 20–40% of your hourly CPVH rate for the hours it's displayed — not a flat fee independent of your audience size or stream length.

**Is a chat callout worth charging for separately?**
Yes — typically 15–25% of your hourly rate per callout. Individually cheap, but a brand asking for several stacks into real money, which is exactly why it should be itemized rather than folded into "just a shoutout."

**What's the difference between VOD reach and usage rights?**
VOD reach is your sponsorship staying visible in your own VOD after the stream — price it as an add-on to the stream rate. Usage rights are the brand reusing your clip in *their* marketing outside your channel — that's a separate licensing fee, priced by where they plan to run it.

**A brand wants five different deliverables for one flat price — how do I respond?**
Price each deliverable against your hourly rate, sum them, and send an itemized quote. You can still offer a bundle discount, but starting from the itemized total means you're negotiating down from your number, not up from theirs.
