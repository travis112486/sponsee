Three emails. Copy them, fill in the brackets, send them on the schedule below. That's the whole system — no negotiation theater, no guessing how firm to sound at each stage.

These pair with [Sponsor Paying Late? What to Do](/blog/sponsor-paying-late), which covers when to send each one and what to do if none of them get a response. This post is just the scripts.

## Template 1 — the friendly nudge

**Send:** on the due date, or up to 3 days after.
**Tone:** assumes good faith. Most late payments are an invoice that got buried, not a brand stiffing you on purpose.

> Subject: Quick check on invoice #[NUMBER]
>
> Hi [NAME],
>
> Hope you're doing well! Just a quick note — invoice #[NUMBER] for [$AMOUNT] (sent [DATE], due [DUE DATE]) shows as outstanding on my end. Totally possible it's just sitting in a queue somewhere, but wanted to flag it in case it slipped through.
>
> Let me know if you need anything else from me to get it processed.
>
> Thanks,
> [YOUR NAME]

## Template 2 — the professional reminder

**Send:** 7–10 days past the due date, if template 1 got no response (or a response with no follow-through).
**Tone:** still polite, but names the date explicitly and asks for a specific answer.

> Subject: Following up — invoice #[NUMBER], [X] days overdue
>
> Hi [NAME],
>
> Following up on invoice #[NUMBER] for [$AMOUNT], which was due [DUE DATE] and is now [X] days past due. I haven't seen payment come through or heard back on my note from [DATE OF TEMPLATE 1].
>
> Could you confirm either (a) it's been paid and I'm missing the notification, or (b) a new date I can expect it? Happy to resend the invoice or provide any documentation your AP team needs.
>
> Thanks for helping me get this sorted,
> [YOUR NAME]

## Template 3 — the escalation

**Send:** 14–21 days past due, if template 2 got no response or no real answer.
**Tone:** firm and factual. State a consequence you're actually prepared to follow through on — don't bluff.

> Subject: Overdue invoice #[NUMBER] — [X] days past due, response needed
>
> Hi [NAME],
>
> Invoice #[NUMBER] for [$AMOUNT] is now [X] days past its [DUE DATE] due date. I've followed up twice ([DATE], [DATE]) without a resolution.
>
> I need a firm payment date by [SPECIFIC DATE — give 3–5 business days]. If I don't hear back, I'll need to [pause future deliverables under our current agreement / escalate to (a manager, finance contact, or collections) — pick the one you'll actually do].
>
> I'd rather resolve this directly — let me know how you'd like to proceed.
>
> [YOUR NAME]

## How to use these well

- **Fill in every bracket.** A template with placeholders left in reads as careless, which undercuts the "I run this like a business" signal you're going for.
- **cc a second contact once you reach template 3**, if you have one (a manager, a general inquiries address) — brand contacts change roles and go on leave, and your invoice shouldn't die with someone's inbox.
- **Keep the tone escalation gradual, not a straight line to angry.** Template 3 is firm, not hostile — you may work with this brand again, and "professional but serious" gets paid faster than "furious" does.
- **Don't skip steps.** Jumping straight to escalation on day one reads as disproportionate for what's very often an innocent delay, and it burns a relationship you might not need to burn.

**How do I ask a brand for late payment without sounding rude?**
Start with the friendly-nudge template — it assumes good faith and just flags the invoice, which is how most late payments actually get resolved. Save firmer language for the escalation stage, and only after two unanswered follow-ups.

**How many days should I wait between reminder emails?**
Roughly a week between each: nudge at the due date, professional reminder around day 7–10 past due, escalation around day 14–21.

**Should I cc anyone on the escalation email?**
If you have a second contact at the brand — a manager or general inquiries address — cc them once you reach the escalation stage. It reduces the chance the thread dies in one person's inbox.

**What if I don't get a response to the escalation email either?**
That's covered in the companion post on [what to do when a sponsor doesn't pay](/blog/sponsor-paying-late) — short version: give it 5–7 business days, then it moves from "chasing" to a collections or write-off decision.
