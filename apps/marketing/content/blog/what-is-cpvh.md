Every pricing page on this site uses one number: CPVH. If you landed here wondering what that actually stands for — or why live-sponsorship pricing doesn't work like the follower-count math you see everywhere else — this is the definition.

## CPVH: cost per viewer-hour

**CPVH stands for cost per viewer-hour.** The unit it prices is one person watching your stream for one hour — a "viewer-hour." CPVH is simply the price of one of those units.

The formula that uses it:

> **Average CCV × sponsored hours × CPVH rate = your quote range**

Published benchmarks vary by source: Gamesight puts general Twitch-sponsorship CPVH at **$0.60–$1.00**,[^2] while Launchpoint's data — skewed toward mid-tier and agency-represented deals — runs **$1.00–$2.00**.[^1] This site uses **$0.60–$1.50** as its working band for the formula below: not a figure either source states outright, but the range that brackets both. That band — not any single published number — is what "CPVH" refers to when you see it elsewhere on this site. (For the full worked-example walkthrough of turning your own CCV and stream length into a quote, see [how much to charge for a sponsored stream](/blog/how-much-to-charge-sponsored-stream); for the same band applied across channel sizes, see [Twitch sponsorship rates](/blog/twitch-sponsorship-rates).)

## Why live needed its own metric

Every other form of creator sponsorship — a sponsored post, a YouTube integration, a TikTok video — has a fixed unit: one piece of content, delivered once, to a countable audience. That's why influencer pricing runs on **CPM (cost per thousand impressions or followers)**: a stable denominator exists, so a stable rate can sit on top of it.

Live doesn't have that unit. A sponsored segment might run for 20 minutes or 4 hours. The audience is real-time and changes minute to minute — someone who tunes in at minute 5 and leaves at minute 40 saw a different sponsorship than someone who was there the whole stream. There's no single "impression" to count, so there's no clean way to apply CPM math to a live sponsorship without losing the thing that actually matters: how long people were actually watching.

CPVH solves that by pricing the dimension live streaming actually has — concurrent viewers, sustained over time — instead of forcing live into a metric built for static content. That's the whole reason the term exists, and it's why a follower count (or a CPM figure quoted from a brand's Instagram media plan) is the wrong number to negotiate from.

## Why brands show up thinking in CPM anyway

Most brand-side marketers doing sponsorships buy the rest of their media in CPM. It's the ad-industry default, so it's the instinct they bring to a live-sponsorship conversation even when it's the wrong tool for the job. You'll sometimes see it surface as "what's your CPM for this stream?" or a budget framed around impressions.

You don't need to argue the marketing-theory point. You need one sentence that redirects the conversation to the number that actually applies: *"Live doesn't have a fixed impression count the way a post does — I price it on concurrent-viewer-hours instead, which is the standard live-sponsorship metric. At my average CCV, that's a range of $X–$Y."* That reframes the ask without requiring the brand to admit anything — you're just supplying the metric that fits the format, and most media buyers who've bought live before will recognize CPVH immediately once you say it.

## How to calculate your effective CPVH from a deal you already closed

CPVH isn't only a quoting tool. Run the formula backward on a deal you've already been paid for, and it tells you whether you were priced fairly — in dollars, not a feeling.

> **Deal amount ÷ (average CCV × sponsored hours) = your effective CPVH**

**Example 1.** You averaged 600 CCV, ran a 3-hour dedicated sponsored stream, and were paid a flat $700.

Effective CPVH = $700 ÷ (600 × 3) = $700 ÷ 1,800 = **$0.39/viewer-hour.**

That's below the $0.60 floor of the published band. Not a guess, not a hunch — a number you can hold up next to the benchmark and say "this deal underpriced me by roughly a third against the floor of the published range."

**Example 2.** You averaged 1,200 CCV, ran a 4-hour stream, and were paid $3,600.

Effective CPVH = $3,600 ÷ (1,200 × 4) = $3,600 ÷ 4,800 = **$0.75/viewer-hour.**

That lands comfortably mid-band — evidence the deal was priced reasonably, and a real number to anchor your renewal ask at ("last time we ran at roughly $0.75/viewer-hour, and I'd want to hold that floor for this campaign").

**Do this with your last three deals.** If your effective CPVH is consistently under $0.60, you have hard evidence you're leaving money on the table with every brand you work with — not just this one. If it's mid-band or higher, you have a number worth defending on your next renewal instead of re-negotiating from scratch.

## What moves your CPVH — briefly

Where a specific deal lands inside $0.60–$1.50 isn't random: audience-brand fit, exclusivity, usage rights, multi-platform simulcast reach, and engagement quality (chat activity, returning-viewer share) all push a quote up or down. That's a full topic on its own — see the [pricing method pillar](/blog/how-much-to-charge-sponsored-stream) for the complete list with negotiation language for each one.

## FAQ

*(FAQPage schema at publish)*

**What does CPVH stand for?**
Cost per viewer-hour — the price of one person watching your stream for one hour. It's the pricing unit for live-stream sponsorships, the way CPM (cost per thousand impressions) is the pricing unit for feed content.

**Is CPVH the same as CPM?**
No. CPM prices a fixed count of impressions or followers reached once. CPVH prices sustained concurrent attention over time, which is what a live sponsorship actually delivers. Brands sometimes ask for a "CPM" on a live sponsorship out of habit; CPVH is the metric that actually fits the format.

**What's a good CPVH?**
Published sources vary: Gamesight puts general Twitch-sponsorship CPVH at $0.60–$1.00, while Launchpoint's mid-tier/agency-deal data runs $1.00–$2.00. This site's working band — the range those two sources bracket — is $0.60–$1.50. Where you land in that range depends on audience fit, exclusivity, usage rights, and engagement — see the pricing method pillar for the full breakdown.

**How do I find my average CCV?**
Twitch's Channel Analytics, YouTube Studio, and Kick's creator dashboard all report average concurrent viewers per stream. Use a trailing 30-day average across normal streams, not your best night, and combine platforms if you simulcast.

**How do I calculate my effective CPVH from a past deal?**
Divide what you were paid by (your average CCV × the sponsored hours). If the result is under $0.60, that deal priced below the published floor; mid-band or above means it was priced fairly against the benchmark.

**Does CPVH apply on YouTube Live, Kick, and TikTok Live?**
The published band is drawn primarily from Twitch-centric agency data, but the unit — a concurrent-viewer-hour of live attention — is platform-agnostic. Treat it as your starting benchmark anywhere you stream, and use your combined CCV when you simulcast.
